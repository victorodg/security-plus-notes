# Security+ Notes — Basic Risk Concepts

Tags: #security-plus #fundamentals #risk-management

## Why Risk Matters

One of the basic goals of implementing IT security is to **reduce risk**.

**Risk** is the possibility or likelihood of a threat exploiting a vulnerability, resulting in a loss.

---

## Core Definitions

**Threat**
Any circumstance or event that has the potential to compromise confidentiality, integrity, or availability.

**Vulnerability**
A weakness. This can exist in hardware, software, configuration, or even in the users operating the system.

**Security Incident**
An adverse event, or series of events, that can negatively affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of an organization's IT systems and data. This includes intentional attacks, malicious software infections, accidental data loss, and more.

---

## Categorizing Threats

Threats can be classified along a few different dimensions:

| Dimension | Types |
|---|---|
| **Origin** | Internal (e.g., a malicious insider) vs. External (e.g., an attacker anywhere in the world) |
| **Source** | Natural (e.g., hurricanes, floods) vs. Human-made (e.g., malware written by a criminal) |
| **Intent** | Intentional (e.g., attackers) vs. Accidental (e.g., employee mistakes, system errors) |

---

## Risk Mitigation

**Risk mitigation** reduces either:
- the chances that a threat will exploit a vulnerability, or
- the impact the risk will have on the organization if it does occur

You reduce risk by implementing **controls** — also called **countermeasures** or **safeguards**.

**Example:** Access controls ensure only authorized personnel can access specific areas, systems, or data — reducing the risk of unauthorized disclosure or tampering.

---

## Quick Recap
- **Risk** = likelihood of a threat exploiting a vulnerability → loss
- **Threat** = potential event that could compromise C-I-A
- **Vulnerability** = a weakness (hardware, software, config, or human)
- **Controls/countermeasures/safeguards** = what you implement to mitigate risk
